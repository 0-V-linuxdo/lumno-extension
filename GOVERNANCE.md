# Lumno Governance

Lumno is a community-facing open source project maintained in public. This document describes how technical, operational, and financial decisions are made.

## Roles

### Maintainers

Maintainers review contributions, manage releases, operate project infrastructure, and make final decisions when consensus cannot be reached. Maintainer access is granted based on sustained, trusted participation and may be removed when it is no longer required.

### Contributors

Anyone may propose changes through public issues and pull requests. Contributions are evaluated on their technical merit, project fit, maintainability, security, and user impact.

### Sponsors

Sponsors provide financial support but receive no governance rights, administrative access, preferential review, or control over the roadmap. The same public contribution process applies to sponsors and non-sponsors.

## Decision-making

Routine changes are discussed and reviewed through issues and pull requests. Maintainers seek consensus where practical. When consensus cannot be reached, the maintainers responsible for the affected area make the final decision and document material tradeoffs publicly.

Changes that materially affect licensing, governance, user privacy, project ownership, or financial policy should receive a public discussion period before adoption.

## Project assets and access

Repositories, release credentials, browser-store accounts, domains, infrastructure, and project secrets are managed solely for Lumno's open source work. Access follows least-privilege principles. Where practical, recovery access should be shared with at least two trusted maintainers to reduce continuity risk.

Project credentials must not be used for unrelated personal, client, or commercial activity.

## Financial governance

Project funding and spending follow [SPONSORING.md](SPONSORING.md) and use the official funding channel published there. Expenses must support completed or clearly documented open source work.

Administrators must disclose conflicts of interest. A sponsor, employer, or client cannot purchase a technical decision, private deliverable, endorsement, or governance role through project funding.

## Commercial separation

If a maintainer or third party offers commercial services related to Lumno, those services must be clearly identified as separate from the open source project. They must use separate contracts, finances, infrastructure, credentials, and paid operational accounts.

## Continuity

If active maintainers become unavailable, trusted contributors may propose a transfer of responsibilities through a public issue. Repository, release, infrastructure, and financial access should be transferred in a documented manner that preserves the project's open source purpose.
